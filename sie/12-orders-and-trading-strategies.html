12. Orders and Trading Strategies

Trade Capacity

Broker (Agent): A firm that acts as a broker finds the other side of the trade on behalf of its client. They are finding buyers for seller and sellers for buyers FOR their customer

  • They earn a commission for its effort, a seperate dollar amount per trade on the trade confirmation

Dealers (Principals): When a firm buys or sells securities for or from its own account

  • A dealer that is ready to buy/sell a specific stock is a market maker for that stock
    • Market makers provide a 2 sided quote, a bid (what its willing to buy for) and a ask price (what its willing to sell for)
      • Example: $19.90 - $20.25 is a bid of $19.90 or less, or ask of $20.25 or more, the difference is the spread, their profit.
      • Bids and offers are typically posted in round lots. For securities priced at <=$250, lot size is 100, for >$250, the lot size reduces in proportion to price
  • Dealers adjust prices for retail customers (markup and markdown), calculated from inside market (the highest big and lowest ask from all market makers)

Dealers assume risk, are principals, and earn markup/markdown

Brokers don't assume risk, earn on commission, they are an agent for customer

FINRA enforces a "fair limit of" maximum markup/markdown/commission of 5%. (divide markup by prevailing price)

  • Only a guideline though, depends on the security, its availability, price (lower prices are charged more), amount of money, disclosure, nature of the business

Proceeds Transaction: When a customer directs a member firm to sell a security, and use its proceed to buy another security

  • You can't double dip your commission (if you buy an sell for $5000, you can only charge max 5% of $5000, not $10,000)

Securities that require delivery of prospectus are exempt bc they are sold at a specific public offering price

  • Examples of the securities that are exempt include initial public offerings, municipal bonds, and mutual fund shares.

Discretionary Orders: Clients can give registered representatives discretion to make orders/trades

  • The RR should mention whether or not they used discretions (if client consented or not)
  • and if it was solicited (clients idea or theirs)

Types of Transactions

When buying securities, one may:

  • Buy by paying in full
  • Buy with borrowed funds (on margin)

When selling securities, one may:

  • Sell what they own -> long sale
  • Sell what they don't own (borrowed) -> short sale
    • The seller will need to buy back the shares in the future to return to the lender
    • They make profit if the price in the future is lower than the price when borrowed (bearish)
    • Short sales must be executed in a margin account
      • Brokerage firms give short sellers shares from other margin accounts that have provided permission
      • As long as short sellers account maintains required margin equity, theres no set time they must repurchase borrowed shares
      • If cash dividend is paid on borrowed stock, the borrower must pay it to the lender

Market Order: client doesn't specify a price, it gets executed at best price available once entered, usually used for liquid stocks

Limit Order: client wants to buy/sell at a specific price, will only execute at specified price OR better - takes longer than market order

  • Buy limit order will be at that price or lower
  • Sell limit order will be at that price or higher
  • Used for illiquid stocks or big order, there is a risk order is not executed

Stop (Loss) Orders: Contingent order, executed when market price of a stock rises or falls to specified price. If it reaches that price (stop price), it activates and becomes a market order - no guarantees on price of execution

  • Sell Stop: Placed below current market price - used to limit a loss on a long stock position
  • Buy Stop: Placed above -- used by short sellers

Stop Limit Order: Similar to Stop order, but instead of activating as market order, it activates as a limit order w specified price

  • May not receive execution, unlike stop order

Day Orders: Good for the day (9:30AM to 4:00PM ET) - cancels at end of day

Good Till Cancelled (GTC) / Open Order: Remains in effect on broker-dealers order book until either executed or cancelled

  • Can be any specified length of time
  • Must be updated with partial fills
