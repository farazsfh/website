16. Prohibited Activities

Manipulation: Securities Exchange act of 1934 prohibits manipulative/deceptive practices when selling securities

  • Rule 10b-1 and 10b-3 state it applies to even SEC registration exempt securities, and broker-dealers

Misrepresentation: SEC registration does not imply approval, it is manipulative to say so

Regulation M: Issuers and underwriting syndicates may not aggressively bid for or make purchases of a security on the secondary market thats actively being distributed

  • They also may not promise to repurchase shares of an IPO (laddering)

Market Rumours: You are not allowed to spread rumours to inflate/deflate the price of a security (pump and dump)

Front Running: When a BD or others know about a block trade (10k shares or more) that hasn't been executed they may not place trades in that security or related securities to profit off of the knowledge. They must wait until full block trade has been made public.

Trading Ahead of a Research: Firms may not trade based on research reports not yet made public

  • Research and trading departments are separated w/ information barriers
  • Applies to exchange and non-exchange listed securities

Churning: You may not trade excessively in a customers account to generate fees

Marking the Open/Close: You may not make a conscious transaction to uptick/downtick the security right at or within minutes of open/close. This can affect margin requirements, other indexes etc.

Backing Away: Market makers must honor their quotes (price and size). If they back away from a transaction based on their quote, they can get a fine or suspension of market making activities

Freeriding: Buying a security but not paying for it within S + 2 (T + 3), waiting for price to go up, and then telling BD to sell security and use proceeds to pay for the costs of initial buying.

  • If customer doesn't pay in S + 2, their account gets frozen for 90 days, where they have to pay for transactions in advance

FINRA Rules:

  • Anti Intimidation/Coordination: You cannot coordinate prices/quotes, trades etc. with others, you many not request another member to alter prices, harrass or coerce others, retaliate against others in the market
  • Payments to Influence Market Price: You may not pay any party to influence the price (like media outlets). Advertising and research reports are fine
  • Best Execution: BD must use reasonable diligence to get customers best price on purchases and sales
  • Time of Trade Disclosures (MSRB Rule G-47): All material info must be disclosed at or before purchase of municipal securities (they have weird features usually)
    • Cannot satisfy this by just redirecting customer to established industry source or generic advertising material
  • Interpositioning: BD cannot put another firm in between transaction to the detriment of customer (they do this to make extra markup)
    • Allowed when truly required or customer benefits (lack of personnel is not a suitable reason)
  • Trading Ahead of Customer Orders (Limit Order Protection Rule): A firm may not accept a customers order and then make an order that is the same side and same price as the order. If they do, they must fill customers order in 60 seconds or less.
    • Exception only if different department made order, and info barriers are in place
  • Quoting a Security in Multiple Mediums: BDs must display same quote if quoting in multiple markets
  • Mutual Fund Trading Rule: A mutual fund is allowed to buy/sell securities from another mutual fund without being subject to trade restrictions IF transactions are minor and strictly between portfolio affiliates or between IC and affiliate persons (a mutual fund can purchase securities from another mutual fund if both funds are controlled by the same investment company.)

Insider Trading: Trading using material, non public information in a fraudulent manner

  • Both tippers and tippees are liable
  • The Insider Trading Sanctions Act of 1984 (ITSA) and the Insider Trading and Securities Fraud Enforcement Act of 1988 (ITSFEA) created to punish insider trading
  • Broker dealers must establish and enforce written policies and procedures to prevent misuse of information by firms and associated persons
    • Must have system to monitor employee personal trading, restricting employees from buying securities in which firm has interest, education etc.
  • Information barriers must be present
  • Restricted and watch lists: firms with IB, research and arbitrage activities must have restricted and watch lists. If any material non public info is known about a company, it must be added to watch list.
  • Consequences
    • Civil penalties - SEC can sue for up to 3x gain or possible loss avoided, return of profits earned, managers and supervisors can also be sued for max of treble damages or 1 mil
    • Criminal - fines of up to $5 million and/or prison for up to 20yrs per violation, corps can be fined up to $25m per violation, DOJ enforces these punishments
    • Bounties - SEC may grant bounties for tips - reward usually 10-30% of money collected

New Issue Rule: Brokerage firms are required to make bonafide offering of new issues and not withhold any shares for its own account, or employee accounts. Prohibits a FINRA member broker-dealer from selling a new issue to an account in which a restricted person has a beneficial interest.

  • Personnel of limit broker-dealers (e.g those who only sell mutual funds are exempt)
  • The following are NOT new issues:
    • Secondary offering
    • Private offering
    • Debt offerings
    • Preferred stock/rights offering
    • etc.

Sharing: Employees of FINRA and MSRB firms cannot share in profits/losses with customers unless:

  • Prior consent given by both parties
  • Profit/losses are shared in proportion to their financial deposits
  • Investment Advisory Accounts are exempt if consent given
  • Employees of member firms may neither guarantee against losses in customer accounts nor reimburse a customer for any losses

Borrowing/Lending: A registered individual may not borrow or lend money to a customer while they have that relationship AND 6 months after their relationship ends too. Unless:

  • They are immediate family members or live together and supported financially
  • OR customer is a financial institution that regularly engages in providing credit/loans etc.
  • OR both people are registered with same firm
  • OR both have a bona fide arrangement from before the BD <-> customer relationship began
  • OR arrangement is based on a bona fide business relationship outside of BD <-> customer

For 3,4,5 -> registered person must inform firm in writing before entering arrangement. Firm must keep file of all arrangements for atleast 3 yrs before it ends or 3 yrs after BD leaves firm

Financial Exploitation of Specified Adults – FINRA Rule 2165

  • Specified Adults: seniors age 65+ or mentally retarded adults 18+
  • Trusted Contact Person: if firm believes financial exploitation is happening, they can contact them before any funds or securities are dispersed. its not required to open an account
  • Financial Exploitation: Trying to take control of, or misuse, money, securities, account etc.
  • Temporary Hold: Firms can put temporary hold on all transactions and disbursement of securities or funds. They can block all movement, even within brokerage. They must allow normal bill paying etc. They must investigate
  • Notification: Within 2 business days firm must notify of hold to all parties named on account and trusted contact person unless one of them is committing the exploit
  • Holds expire 15 business days unless terminated or extended (additional 10 business days, then, an additional 30 business days)

Outside Accounts: Before an outside account is opened, the employees are required to provide written notification to the executing firm of their association with another member firm. Firms need to monitor outside accounts of employees

  • Also applied to accounts where employee has beneficial interest (spouse accounts, children, related persons etc.)
  • For previously opened accounts, employee must notify employer within 30 days of starting employment
  • Upon request dupliocate copies of confirmations, statements will be sent to employer
  • The requirements of this rule don’t apply to accounts that are limited to transactions involving redeemable investment company securities (mutual fund shares), unit investment trusts, variable contracts, or 529 plans.

Payments: A BD cannot pay any compensation to another BD or associated person if they're not properly registered (commissions, discounts, concessions on issues etc.)

  • If a contract is created, a member firm can continue to pay commissions to retiring representatives after they leave, but only in existing accounts and they mustn't solicit new business or open new accounts

Forgery: Don't commit forgery, even accidentally

Books/Records: BDs must maintain records in either paper, micrographic media or electronic

  • If using electronic, must notify regulator prior to use, and if they change form of electronic media, must notify regulator 90 days prior
    • For electronic media:
      • Must be non re-writeable and non erasable
      • Automatically confirm quality/accuracy
      • Maintain serial format with timestamps
      • Be able to download indexes and records to an SEC approved format

Micrographic media must have duplicates in a separate location and locations must be accessible to SEC and firms SRO.

For FINRA, the requirements are found in Rule 4511; however, for the MSRB, the requirements are found in Rule G-8 (the records that must be kept) and Rule G-9 (how long the records must be kept).

CRD Updates: If certain events occur, member firm must file report w/ FINRA within 30 days

  • If customer complaint involves allegations of theft, forgery etc.
  • Member has been found to violate securities laws or rules of an SRO, government etc
  • Member has been denied registration, or expelled by a financial regulating body
  • Has been named as a defendant in any proceeding brought by domestic/foreign regulatory body alleging violation of securities etc regulation
  • Any financial crimes
  • Part of a financial org that was denied/expelled from financial org
  • Is a defendent in arbitration that resulted in <=$15k for persons or >=$25k for firms
  • Suspended or terminated by firm or fined in excess of $2500

Quarterly Reports: FINRA members must send quarterly reports by 15th of month following end of quarter in which complaints were filed (unless no complaints)

Confidential Settlement of Complaints: The terms of a customer’s settlement against a broker-dealer may be confidential but registered person still has to report to CRD

Red Flags: Suspicious behavior. When supervisor sees one:

  • Investigate the situation, document the investigation, pursue the investigation to a conclusion
  • Document everything, take into account prior conduct

Outside Business Activities: Things like second jobs - RR must give written notice before doing this - to avoid conflict of interest

Private Securities Transactions: Person must give written notice before doing this. If they do it without giving notice, it is called selling away

  • Things like: private placements, public offerings, arranging loans etc
  • If they make money off of it -> the member must specifically approve transaction
  • If not -> they still must be notified
  • Personal transactions involving investment company and variable annuity securities are not covered by this rule.

Gifts: Members gift limit is $300 per year to any person, principal, employee or rep of another firm if the payment is in relation to business of employer. Doesn't apply to employer <-> employee gifts or gifts to coworkers.

  • If given to a group, divide by size of group
  • For tickets, value is max of face value or purchase value
  • Personal gifts (wedding, baby etc) are exempt from $300 limit
  • Tiny (de minimis) or promotional or commemorative gifts are also exempt
  • Business entertainment is exempt if:
    • It is not frequent
    • Member hosts the client/guest (accompany them)
    • Travel and lodging included
  • A firm that creates mutual funds can't pay another broker-dealer's commission in stocks or options. Commissions get paid in cash, through the firm.
  • As a registered rep, you can only be paid by your own firm. Nobody else can pay you for selling DPPs, REITs, mutual funds, or variable products (variable annuities, variable life), in cash or anything else.
