**Issuer**: An entity that sells securities in order to finance its operations (business or government)
- They issue either 
	- **Debt securities** (a bond, which is a publicly traded loan that promises a repayment of the principal on a specific date, plus usually interest according to a schedule)
	- **Equity securities** aka stocks, an ownership interest in the business. No maturity date, and may pay out dividends from profits

**Broker**: any person that engages in the business of effecting _agency_ transactions in securities for the account of *others*
- Matches up buyers and sellers to earn commission on sale 

**Dealer**: any person that engages in the business of buying and selling securities for its own account
- Buys securities from clients, holds them in inventory, and sells them to clients
- Makes money from markup/markdown (buy low sell high)

Broker-Dealers do both of these. There are a few departments as well:
- **Investment Bankers**: works directly with the issuers to arrange and structure their securities offerings, help with mergers and acquisitions etc. The *underwriters* of securities.
- **Research**: analyze market and securities - recommend buy, hold or sell
- **Sales**: marketers of securities to retail investors or institutions, aka *registered representatives (RRs) or investment adviser representatives (IARs)*
- **Trading**: handle execution of trades
- **Operations**: ensures that all of the paperwork, funds, and securities transfers that are associated with a trade (or processing) are handled efficiently and according to specific industry standards

**Market Maker**: A broker-dealer that chooses to display quotes into a trading system
- **Bid**: price they are willing to buy security for
- **Ask**: price they are willing to sell security for
- **Size**: number of shares willing to buy/sell
A market maker is required to make regular bids and offers and meet specific capital requirements.

**Trader**: Firms/people who buy/sell securities for their own benefit. No obligation to enter quotes into a marketplace, they use market makers quotes.

**Investment Advisor**: someone who charges _fees_ for providing advice to their clients. Usually based on a percentage of AUM. 
- <$100M AUM = register w state(s)
- $100M - $110 AUM = register w state(s) OR SEC
- >$100M AUM = register with SEC
A **municipal advisor** is an investment advisor that works with municipalities to provide advice on securities they want to issue etc.

**Retail Investors**: regular people who buy securities from broker-dealers directly

**Accredited Investors**: viewed as more sophisticated and/or able to assume greater risk
- Banks, private corporations
- Directors, CEOS
- People with certain FINRA professional certification (Series 7, 65, 82 or CFP)
- Net worth of at least $1,000,000 (excluding their primary residence) OR gross annual income of at least $200,000 (or $300,000 combined with a spouse) for each of the past two years

**Institutional Investors**: Large entities that pool their money to purchase securities OR individual with >$50M assets.

**Qualified institutional buyers (QIBS):** An institution, not an individual, must meet this test
1. First, only certain types of investors are eligible, including:
    - Insurance companies
    - Registered investment companies
    - Registered investment advisers
    - Small business development companies
    - Private and public pension plans
    - Certain bank trust funds
    - Corporations, partnerships, business trusts, and certain non-profit organizations
2. The buyer must be purchasing for its own account or for the account of another QIB.
3. The buyer must own and invest at least $100 million of securities of issuers that are not affiliated with the buyer.

**Primary Market**: Where the issuer sells to public investors for the first time
**Secondary Market**: Where investors buy/sell those initial securities without the issuer involved

**Exchange**: A place (physical or digital) where trading happens, auction market
**OTC equities or unlisted securities**: Over the counter, meaning they're not listed on an exchange. Trades occur in non-physical dealer-to-dealer networks that connect participants
- The following are tiers of OTC markets:
	- **OTCQX Best Market** - almost like an exchange
	- **OTCQB Venture Market** - annual verification, min bid price of $0.01
	- **OTCID Basic Market** - basic info provided
	- **Pink Limited Market** - issuer not involved

There is no exchange for corporate/municipal/US gov bonds, most bonds are traded in the OTC market through various dealer-to-dealer networks.

**The Third Market**: refers to exchange-listed securities being traded over-the-counter or away from traditional exchanges. Involves transactions between dealer-brokers and large institutions.

**The Fourth Market**: direct institution-to-institution trading and doesn’t involve the public markets or exchanges. Involve proprietary trading systems (PTSs)

**Electronic Communication Networks (ECNs)**: Allow for quoting and trading of exchange listed securities. These systems allow subscribers to disseminate information about orders, execute transactions both during the trading day and after-hours, and buy and sell anonymously. ECNs charge subscribers a fee for using their systems and act in only an agency (broker) capacity.

**Dark Pools**: system that provides liquidity for large institutional investors and high-frequency traders, but it doesn’t disseminate quotes.
- Used when investors want to buy/sell large chunks and reduce market impact
- Remain anonymous

**The Depository Trust & Clearing Corporation (DTCC)**: National clearinghouse, non-profit, industry-owned corporation. Its owners include broker-dealers, investment banks, commercial banks, and mutual fund companies.

**Clearing Firms**: broker-dealers that perform order execution, clearing, and settlement functions. Clearing firms interface with the DTCC directly

**Introducing Firms**: neither process customer transactions nor do they operate their own clearing operations. Instead, they contract with clearing firms to perform these services. customer funds and securities are actually physically held at the clearing firm
- **Fully Disclosed Accounts**: the clearing firm receives all info about customer, have separate accounts etc.
- **Omnibus Accounts**: the introducing firm will have 1 account for all of its customers, so the clearing firm doesn't receive discrete customer info, introducing firm handles that paperwork

**Hedge Funds**: a private, actively managed investment fund that uses sophisticated strategies in an attempt to generate returns

**Prime Brokerage Accounts**: A customer can choose one clearing firm as their prime broker. This prime broker will consolidate trade data from any executing broker the customer uses and handle the bookkeeping, and provide reports to customer.

**Options**: Options are derivative trading products that track the value of an individual stock, an index, or foreign currency. Traded on options exchanges or OTC. there is standardization, which means that the terms of the contracts are set and uniform. 

The DTCC doesn’t clear options trades; instead, this is the job of the Options Clearing Corporation (OCC). When customers buy or sell option contracts, their broker-dealers must settle the transactions with the OCC within one business day.

**Custodians**: banks or other financial institutions that hold customers' securities for safekeeping, physically or in book entry/electronic form

**Registrars**: maintains the ownership register of the issuer for each issue of its securities
- records the name, address, and tax identification or Social Security number of each individual owner
ONLY the registrar ensures that the number of issued shares doesn’t exceed the number of authorized shares.

**Transfer Agent**: Responsible for
- The issuance and cancelation of certificates to reflect changes in ownership
- Maintaining a list of current shareholders who are eligible to receive additional shares after a stock split
- Acting as the company’s paying agent for interest payments on bonds and for cash or stock dividends on equities
- Acting as proxy agent (sending voting materials) and mailing agent (mailing the company’s financial reports to shareholders)
- Handling lost, destroyed, or stolen certificates

**Securities Trustee**: usually banks or trust companies, that legally hold and administer assets on behalf of others under a trust arrangement
- owns the stuff legally and is responsible for making sure the deal's rules are followed, even against the other parties involved.
