**The SEC**: Securities and Exchange Commission. 
- protects investors, maintains fair/orderly trading markets, facilitates capital formation for primary market
- Jurisdiction across state borders, can investigate law violations, criminal activity is under DOJ

**The Federal Reserve Board (FRB)**: functions as US central bank
- manages US monetary policy (money supply and interest rate)
- GOAL = maximum employment and stable prices

**Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC)**: maintains stability and public confidence in the nation's financial system
- $250k coverage for insurance per depositor 

**Self-Regulatory Organizations (SROs)**: Promotes fair trading activity
- Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (FINRA), the Municipal Securities Rulemaking Board (MSRB), and Chicago Board Options Exchange (CBOE)
- Cannot arrest or imprison anyone
- Firms are required to join one (referred to as member firms, ppl are associated persons)

**State (Blue-Sky) Regulation**: States can have additional regulation
- Broker-Dealers and agents must register in state to do business there, issuers must register their securities in state to sell them there

**Uniform Securities Act** are established by the **North American Securities Administrators Association (NASAA)** and enforced by states. States can add more regulations on top of the USA. The person in charge of the state securities regulation department is their Administrator or Commissioner.

**Firm Rules**: basically just the in-company rules
- Policies are defined in Written Supervisory Procedures (WSP)
- 3 categories: personnel—registered principals, registered representatives (RRs), and unregistered employees (tech, support, administrative etc)

## Acts

**The Securities Act of 1933**: First legislation to cover the securities industry - focused on primary market (where issuers sell to public)
- Demands that investors be provided with FULL and FAIR disclosure so they can make informed decision
- Also details rules for conduct of issuers, and investment bankers (underwriters)

**The Securities Exchange Act of 1934**: Focused on secondary market (such as NYSE and NASDAQ)
- Created the SEC and gave it authority over domestic securities in primary and secondary markets
- Gave the FRB regulatory oversight of the extension of credit (i.e use of margin) in the securities industry
	- margin requirements are established under the provisions of Regulation T
	- a margin account is created for investors who borrow money to purchase securities or to engage in the short selling of securities

**The Maloney Act of 1938**: allowed for the creation of non-exchange SROs
- Most securities transactions are conducted in other venues aside from physical exchanges (OTC markets), The National Association of Securities Dealers (NASD) was created in 1939 to act as the self-regulatory organization for the OTC market
- in 1975, the scope of the Maloney Act enabled the creation of the Municipal Securities Rulemaking Board (MSRB)
- in 2007, the NYSE + NASD merged their regulation functions to form FINRA

**The Investment Advisers Act of 1940**: Defines investment advisers (IAs) w/ ABC test:
- Providing **A**dvice,
- Operating as a **B**usiness, and
- Receiving **C**ompensation for the advice
Exclusions: broker-dealers, professionals, publishers if investment advice is secondary to their actual profession

-> mutual fund must register as a IA

**The Investment Company Act of 1940**: regulates pooled investments managed by IAs. requires face amount certificate companies (FACs), unit investment trusts (UITs), and both open (mutual fund) and closed-end management companies to register with the SEC as investment companies
- funds that have no more than 100 shareholders are not required to register with the SEC
- In order to offer shares publicly, funds must have $100,000 of assets

-> mutual fund must register with SEC

**Securities Investor Protection Act of 1970 (SIPA)**: Created SIPC. SIPC provides insurance coverage for brokerage firm customers to guard against their bankruptcy ONLY
- covers securities registered in street name (held in name of brokerage firm)
- broker-dealers that use mails or interstate commerce are required to join SIPC
- covers a max of $500k cash/securities (no more than $250k may be for cash) per customer (cash and margin accounts are combined)
- join accounts are considered seperate entities
NOT COVERED:
- non-street name securities (these will be distributed to customer, no loss)
- commodities
- personal accounts of senior officers of firm
- other broker-dealers that have securities in the possession of a failed broker-dealer
Insolvency process: bankruptcy -> federal court appoints trustee -> trustee notifies all customers -> securities in possession will be distributed, if insufficient securities available, will be distributed in proportionate basis -> if security can't be identified to be with broker-dealer, market value will be that of the date trustee is appointed
- if customer is over 500k limit, the overage will be how much they can be treated as a general creditor for
FIRMS MUST GIVE CUSTOMERS INFO ABOUT SIPC EVERY YEAR

**The Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974 (ERISA)**
- Covers administrations of private, qualified retirement accounts like 401(k)s

**The Securities Acts Amendments of 1975**
- Created the **Municipal Securities Rulemaking Board (MSRB)** to act as the SRO for firms that work w/ municipal securities
- Uses FINRA or SEC to actually enforce, since it has no enforcement authority

**The Insider Trading and Securities Fraud Enforcement Act of 1988**: Establishes criminal penalties for insider trading, including fines up to $5M and/or 20yrs in jail
- Civilly, SEC may sure for up to 3x profit or loss avoided (treble damages)

**The Penny Stock Reform Act of 1990**: Penny stocks are securities that sell for <$5 per share.
- Act requires firms to obtain signed disclosure docs from potential buyers stating they understand risks (penny stocks are volatile and have low liquidity)

**The Federal Telephone Consumer Protection Act of 1991**: Affects cold callers from people who have profit motive. They must maintain a do not call list and refrain from calling anyone who asks to be placed on it. Calls may only be made 8:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m. local time of the called party

**The USA PATRIOT Act of 2001**: 
- anti money laundering
- Currency Transaction Reports (CTRs) for transactions exceeding $10,000
- Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) for transactions equal to or exceeding $5,000
- Customer Identification Program (CIP)

**FINRA**: primary SRO for securities industry

**The Municipal Securities Rule making Board (MSRB)**: Regulates municipal securities
- Doesn't apply to issuers of municipal securities though
- Rules are enforced by SEC or FINRA for broker-dealers

**The Chicago Board Options Exchange (CBOE)**: Trading venue for options and SRO for options market
- Regulated by SEC and owned by CBOE global markets

**_Code of Procedure (COP)_**: The COP covers the process used to discipline any person that violates FINRA rules
- Can appeal

**_Code of Arbitration_**    The Code of Arbitration provides a process for resolving disputes between members, as well as those that involve public customers. Generally, arbitration is used to settle monetary disputes.
- Cannot appeal
- Customers involved
